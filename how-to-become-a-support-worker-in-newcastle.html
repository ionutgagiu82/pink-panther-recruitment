How to become a support worker in Newcastle and the North East

By John Gagiu, Founder, Pink Panther Recruitment · 21 October 2026 · 4 min read

Support work can be a rewarding job with real flexibility, and there is still steady demand for good people. In 2025/26 there were around 96,000 vacant adult social care posts in England on any given day. Here is how to get started in Newcastle and the wider North East.

What you usually need

  • The right to work in the UK.
  • A DBS check. Care and support roles with vulnerable people need one.
  • References, from employers or, if you are new to work, from teachers or other people who can speak for you.
  • The right attitude. Patience, reliability and honesty matter more than almost anything.

Many support worker roles do not require a formal qualification to start, but training is part of the job. Agencies and providers will normally check that your training is up to date, for example safeguarding, moving and handling and first aid, and help you complete anything missing.

What the work involves

  • Supported accommodation: supporting young people or adults to live independently, with life skills, routines and emotional support.
  • Care homes: personal care and company for older residents.
  • Children's residential: caring for children and young people in children's homes.
  • Learning disability and mental health services: person-centred support in supported living.

Shifts

Shifts can be days, nights, waking nights, sleep-ins or long shifts of 12 to 24 hours. Many people like long shifts and sleep-ins because they mean fewer days at work. It is worth asking each provider or agency how sleep-ins are paid.
